# Why this exists

**A phone that still works isn't obsolete. It's just been abandoned.**

Every year, millions of phones with good screens, fast processors, working touch and USB end up in drawers
or landfill. They aren't broken. The software support stopped, the apps stopped running, or the maker
decided it was time to sell you a new one. This project exists to show that those devices are still
perfectly useful today: a "dead" phone from 2020 boots in seconds, runs for years plugged in without cooking its
battery, and does real work as a panel, a keyboard, a monitor, a server console or whatever you build.

## What we believe
1. **You own your hardware.** If you bought it, you should be able to run any software you like on it,
   including software the maker never imagined.
2. **Bootloaders should be unlockable.** An official, documented unlock is what lets a device outlive its
   support window. Removing OEM unlock, carrier-locking bootloaders, or making unlocking permanently cripple a
   device takes something from owners and sends working hardware to the shredder.
3. **End of support shouldn't mean end of life.** When a maker stops updating a device, the least they can
   do is let its owners keep it running: unlockable bootloaders, published kernel sources (which the GPL
   already requires), and documentation.
4. **Repair and reuse beat recycling.** Recycling a working phone recovers a few grams of metal. Reusing it
   saves the whole device. Every phone ported here is one fewer piece of e-waste.
5. **Knowledge should be shared.** Every quirk found, every port, every test result is written down in this
   repository so the next person starts further ahead.

## What this means for the project
- It's free to use, change, fork and build products on (MIT). We'd rather this spread than control it.
- We ask, but cannot force, that what you build on it stays open and unlockable too. Don't ship it on
  hardware your users can't modify.
- We document honestly: what works, what doesn't, what's risky. Taking control of your hardware means
  knowing what you're doing; we try to make that knowledge easy to get.

## Get involved
Port an old phone, even partially. Report what happened. Support right-to-repair efforts where you live
(for example [iFixit](https://www.ifixit.com/Right-to-Repair) and the EU's right-to-repair rules), and
tell phone makers that unlockable bootloaders matter when you choose what to buy.
